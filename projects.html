CoolDawgs VIPR

This project's aim was to design, develop, and evaluate wearable multi-sensor devices, along with companion mobile apps, to continuously monitor animals' heat stress and notify owners when the risk becomes dangerously high. Working under Dr. Barnes and Ramaswamy, I helped build the UI of our app and developed sensor systems for the hardware.

Our VIPR team's paper, "VIPR CoolDawgs: Building Wearable Sensor and Mobile Application for Canine Heat Stress Detection," was awarded Best Paper in the Technology, Engineering, and Math category at the April 2026 UGA CURO (Center for Undergraduate Research Opportunities) Symposium. Our paper was also one of eight out of 50+ papers to be selected by the JURO (Journal for Undergraduate Research Opportunities) editorial board for publication in the 2026 fall issue!

City Weather Tracker Application

In this weather app I created for CSCI 1302 (Software Development), the user is able to enter the latitude and longitude coordinates of a place and find out what city they correspond to. This is done using the Nominatim API which reverse geocodes an address from a coordinate given latitude and longitude. Then, the user is able to get weather information for that city using the weather-api API which takes the city found earlier to find the weather there!

Airline Baggage Complaint Study

My CSCI 3360 (Data Science and Applied Machine Learning I) team and I analyzed and predicted monthly airline baggage complaints for American Eagle, Hawaiian, and United Airlines using time series data from 2004–2010. We also built and compared FFNN, RNN, and LSTM models using both combined and airline-separated approaches, evaluating performance with RMSE across a chronological 75/25 train-test split.

Urban Heat Planning Analysis

For CSCI 4360 (Data Science and Applied Machine Learning II), my team and I investigated the urban heat island effect by merging global temperature records with city population data across 100 cities spanning 270 years. We also built and compared five predictive models including linear regression and neural networks. From this, we discovered that geography trumps population as the dominant driver of rising urban temperatures.
